Lab 1: Build the Kit in Stages

NoteLab Companion

Pin numbers and parts reflect the current kit. If your term’s handout or iot_pins.h differs, they win.

Goals

  • Assemble the IoT@UCI team kit: two breadboards, the ESP32 and Stage 1 components.
  • Learn to wire carefully and verify each stage before moving on.
  • Understand why the kit is built in stages, just before each part is needed.

Kit assembly is part of the course: you will cut and route wires, solder headers (Lab 0), place parts and test the board yourselves.

The layout

The kit uses two 830-point breadboards side by side along their long edges, with the ESP32 DevKit V1 at the bottom end, bridging the seam so that each row of pins lands on a different board.

  • Left board: LEDs, buttons, potentiometer and buzzer; at the top, the servo and its 470 µF capacitor on a short, separate 5 V rail.
  • Right board: from top to bottom, the ATECC608A, the OLED, the MPU-6050 and the BMP280.
  • A printed holder at the top (download link in Appendix A — Kit Reference) carries the servo and, in some kits, the LoRa radio and its antenna; otherwise the radio sits on the right board.

Figure A.1 in Appendix A shows the finished layout, and Figure A.3 to Figure A.6 show the wiring for each stage.

Run the ESP32’s 3V3 and GND pins to the power rails of both boards. Keep wires short, flat and color-coded: red for 3.3 V, black for ground, a different color for each signal type.

Warning

The servo’s 5 V rail (fed from the ESP32’s VIN pin) must never be connected to the 3.3 V rails. The ESP32’s pins are not 5 V tolerant.

The stages

Stage 1: basic I/O and the display (this lab)

Solder the headers on all breakout boards in this lab, even those you will wire later, so later stages only need wiring.

Stage 2: the I²C bus (before the serial-protocols activity)

All three devices share SDA = GPIO 21 and SCL = GPIO 22, plus 3V3 and GND.

Stage 3: the servo rail (before the actuators activity)

Stage 4: the LoRa radio (before the LoRa activity)

Warning

Solder the antenna to the radio before powering it. Transmitting without an antenna can damage the radio.

Later labs need no new wiring.

Procedure

  1. Inventory your parts against the kit list in Appendix A — Kit Reference. Report anything missing or damaged.
  2. Solder headers on the breakout boards (Lab 0).
  3. Place the breadboards and ESP32, and wire the power rails. With the USB cable connected and nothing else wired, measure 3.3 V between the rails (a TA can help with the multimeter). Unplug again.
  4. Wire Stage 1 one part at a time, following Table 1. After each part, check the wiring against the table with a teammate.
  5. Run the kit check: open the provided kit-check project in PlatformIO, build, upload and open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. It blinks each LED, reads each button and the potentiometer, beeps the buzzer, and writes to the OLED. Fix anything that fails before moving on.
  6. Photograph your finished Stage 1 board from above, and label your kit with your team number.

What to show and submit

  • A TA checkoff of the Stage 1 kit passing the kit check.
  • A top-down photo of the board.
  • The answers to the questions below.

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check
LED never lights LED orientation; resistor in the same row as the LED; correct GPIO
Button always reads pressed or never Button orientation (try rotating it 90°); INPUT_PULLUP in code
Potentiometer reads 0 or 4095 only Wiper on GPIO 34; outer legs on 3V3 and GND
OLED blank All seven wires; SDA on GPIO 23 (not 21); RES and DC not swapped
Upload fails Data-capable USB cable; driver installed; hold BOOT while upload starts
Board resets when the buzzer or servo runs Loose ground; servo on 3.3 V instead of its 5 V rail; missing capacitor

See also Appendix C — Troubleshooting.

Questions

  1. Why are the buttons wired to ground with INPUT_PULLUP, rather than to 3.3 V?
  2. Why is the potentiometer on GPIO 34 rather than an ADC2 pin?
  3. The OLED’s pin is labeled “SDA”, yet it is not connected to the I²C bus. Explain.
  4. Why is the kit built in stages instead of all at once?
